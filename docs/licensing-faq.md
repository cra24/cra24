# Licensing FAQ

The operative documents are [`LICENSE`](../LICENSE) (AGPL-3.0-only) and
[`CLA.md`](../CLA.md), plus the commercial terms, which are sent on request.
[`LICENSING.md`](../LICENSING.md) explains the model. This file answers the
questions people actually ask.

> None of this is legal advice. If the answer matters to your business, have a
> lawyer read the licence rather than this page.

## Using cra24

### I work at a manufacturer. Do we need to pay?

**No.** Running cra24 on your own builds to produce your own compliance dossiers
is free, forever, for any company of any size, under the AGPL.

You are not conveying the software to anyone and you are not offering it over a
network. The AGPL asks nothing of you in that situation.

### We are a 2,000-person company with a proprietary product. Still free?

Still free, for that use. Company size is irrelevant to the licence. What matters
is whether you distribute cra24 or offer it to others as a service.

### Can we modify it for our internal build system?

Yes, and you never have to publish those modifications, as long as you keep them
internal. The AGPL's network clause triggers when *other people* interact with
your modified version over a network, not when your own engineers run it.

Sending them upstream is welcome but not required.

### Does the AGPL infect the products we scan?

**No.** cra24 reads files your build produced. That is not linking, and running a
tool over your source does not place your source under the tool's licence. A
compiler's licence does not bind what you compile.

### Do we own the dossiers, advisories and VEX documents it generates?

**Yes, entirely.** No licence is claimed over cra24's output. The output of a
tool is not a derivative work of the tool. Publish them, sell them, put your own
copyright on them.

### Can we put cra24 in our internal CI?

Yes. Internal CI is internal use. If you expose a web front end onto that CI so
that people outside your organisation can run cra24 through it, that is the
network case — see below.

## When you do need a commercial licence

### We want to ship cra24 inside our product

That is conveying. Under the AGPL you must make the complete corresponding source
of your product available to its recipients, under the AGPL.

If you do not want to do that, [buy a commercial licence](../LICENSING.md#buying-a-commercial-licence).

### We want to offer a hosted compliance service built on cra24

That is AGPL section 13. Your users are entitled to the complete corresponding
source of what you are running, modifications included.

If you do not want to publish it, buy a commercial licence. This is the case the
licence was chosen for.

### Our customer portal shows cra24 output to customers

**Showing output is fine.** A PDF, a dashboard, a downloadable advisory — those
are your documents, generated by a tool you ran.

**Letting customers drive cra24 through your portal is the network case.** The
line is whether the customer is interacting with the software or with its
results.

### We are a consultancy. We run cra24 for our clients and hand them the dossiers

**No licence needed.** You are running it, they are receiving documents. Same as
the question above.

If you build them a portal that runs cra24 on demand, that changes.

### What does a commercial licence cost?

It is negotiated per organisation, and the intended scope metric is the **device
family** — one product line sharing a build configuration and a support period,
however many units ship. Contact the address in [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md).

### Does the licence expire and leave our shipped product infringing?

No. Rights granted for versions released **during** the term are **perpetual**
(commercial licence section 2.1). Expiry ends access to new versions and support;
it does not retroactively make a shipped product infringing.

A subscription that can strand a product already in the field is not something an
embedded manufacturer could responsibly build on, so the licence says so
explicitly.

## Contributing

### Why does cra24 want a CLA? Most projects do not.

Because of the dual licence. Only the copyright holder can offer a work under a
licence other than the one it was received under. A contribution that arrived
under the AGPL alone would make the commercial licence unofferable over any code
containing it — one patch would quietly end the business model.

### Do I give up my copyright?

**No.** You keep it. You grant a parallel licence. You can do anything you like
with your own code, including licensing it to someone else on different terms.

### Can the project go closed-source on me later?

Not for your contribution. **CLA section 7** commits the project to keeping your
contribution available under the AGPL, in a publicly accessible repository, for
as long as it distributes it at all.

That clause is not in the standard Apache ICLA it is adapted from. It is there
because a CLA without it asks a contributor to trust a promise nobody wrote down.

### I do not want to sign a CLA

That is a legitimate position and it is respected. Open an issue describing the
change; a maintainer can implement it independently.

You can also report field spec drift, file bugs, improve documentation discussion
and answer other users' questions without signing anything.

### How do I sign it?

```bash
git commit -s -m "your message"
```

The `Signed-off-by` trailer certifies you have read the CLA and can grant the
licences it describes. Corporations send the schedule in `CLA.md` instead.

## The parts that are not AGPL

| Component | Licence | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generated dossiers, advisories, VEX documents, evidence ledgers | **Yours** | Output is not a derivative work of the tool |
| `docs/` and the regulation data in `src/cra24/data/*.json` | **CC-BY-4.0** | A map of a public regulation should not be enclosed by anyone, including this project |
| CI integrations (planned) | **Apache-2.0** | They are clients; permissive removes every excuse not to adopt them |
| Vendored schemas under `data/schemas/` | Their own terms | See [`NOTICE`](../NOTICE) |

### Can a competitor use the field spec?

Yes. It is CC-BY-4.0, and that is intentional. The field spec is a description of
a public form under a public regulation. Enclosing it would be both wrong and
futile.

The defensible asset is the curated triage feed described in
[roadmap.md](roadmap.md) — sustained human judgement, not a data file.

## Why AGPL rather than something else

**Not Apache-2.0**, because it would let a larger vendor wrap cra24 in a hosted
product, sell it, and contribute nothing back. That has happened to enough
infrastructure projects that the pattern needs no defending.

**Not BUSL or another source-available licence**, because embedded shops have
procurement processes that reject anything not OSI-approved, and cra24 needs to
be installable without a legal review. The AGPL is OSI-approved. BUSL is not.

**Not GPL-3.0**, because the network case is exactly the one that matters here.
The likely competitor is a hosted compliance service, and plain GPL does not
reach it.

The AGPL does not prevent competition. It requires that a competitor who builds
on this work plays by the same rules.

## Trademark

"cra24" is a trademark. You may say factually that a product is "built with
cra24" or "powered by cra24". You may not use the name in a product name, a
domain name, or in any way suggesting endorsement, without written permission.

Forks must be renamed, which is the usual arrangement and is about avoiding
confusion, not about restricting the code.
